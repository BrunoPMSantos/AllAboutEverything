The colophon

About the desk.

All About Everything began in 2021 as a two-person bet that readers still wanted depth — and now runs on curiosity, deadlines, and a shared style guide that forbids the word “delve.”

The first issue of All About Everything went out to 84 people, most of whom were related to the founding editors. It covered the invention of the shipping container, and it ran eleven minutes longer than anyone planned. Fourteen people unsubscribed. Three wrote back to say it was the best thing they had read all year, which evened out nicely.

The premise has not changed since: one subject per week, taken seriously, written by someone who genuinely cares about it. We have covered fungal networks, the Geneva Conventions, competitive topiary, the decline of the semaphore code, and — by sustained popular demand — two consecutive issues about bread. The magazine wins no arguments and aims to keep it that way.

What you read here is edited, fact-checked, and argued over more than any weekly has a right to expect. Every issue ships Thursday, 7 a.m. sharp. If a piece is late, the subject was harder than we thought — a fact we consider a compliment to the subject.

What we believe

Five points, argued internally for months
  1. i.

    Depth over speed

    We would rather be a week late and right than an hour early and glib. Nothing here is written to win the morning.

  2. ii.

    Every subject deserves craft

    There are no small topics, only small attention spans. Moss gets the same edit as monetary policy.

  3. iii.

    Show the working

    Sources, methods, and doubts go in the piece, not in a footnote graveyard. If we cannot explain it plainly, we do not publish it.

  4. iv.

    No pile-ons

    We cover ideas, not people’s worst weeks. The desk has never once used the word “destroyed” in a headline.

  5. v.

    Leave room for delight

    A piece about lighthouse keepers should make you want to visit one. Usefulness without delight is just homework.

Questions we actually get

Answers verified twice
Is anything truly about everything?

Everything, no. Anything, yes. The name is an ambition, not an inventory — like calling a ship Unsinkable, which we do not recommend.

How do you pick the week’s subject?

A suggestion box, a long list, and a Friday argument that ends when someone says “that’s actually fascinating.” Readers suggest about a third of our subjects.

Do you take pitches?

Gladly, from readers and strangers alike. Send three sentences on the subject and why it deserves sixteen patient minutes. Pitches about bread queue-jump automatically.

How is this funded?

Subscriptions, a small flock of patient sponsors, and one very loyal pig farm in Aveiro whose advertisements you have almost certainly seen.

Can I read it without subscribing?

Every piece is free on the site each Thursday. The emailed edition adds a list of four things worth knowing and the satisfying feeling of punctuality.

Any corrections lately?

Issue 41 said the Eiffel Tower grows six meters in summer; it grows roughly 15 centimeters. The error stood for nine hours, which the numbers desk still discusses in group therapy.
